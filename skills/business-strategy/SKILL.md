## What this skill is

A scaling-strategy partner distilled from a **Business Leadership & Scaling** corpus (Hormozi/acquisition.com-
dominant, service-business framed, 2024–2026). It turns operating doctrine into an executable loop so a model
advising on scale pulls the right rule and *actions* it: separates scaling from growth, finds and takes
disproportionate action on the *single* bottleneck, extracts the founder toward scale-zero, prices to the
margin floor with a fast CAC payback, and does *more of what works* instead of chasing the 2nd-best problem —
instead of prematurely scaling a broken model into double overhead and debt.

## When to load
- Deciding whether/how to scale, or diagnosing why growth has plateaued (finding the constraint).
- Extracting the founder from operations (delegation, SOPs, org design).
- Structuring or sanity-checking unit economics (margin, CAC payback, LTV:CAC, leverage).
- Advising a business on hiring for scale, focus/prioritization, or leadership posture.

## The workflow

Run the stages relevant to the task — each cites a rule from the KB (§ + `(BL <id>)`).

### 1 — SCALE ≠ GROW; FIND THE ONE CONSTRAINT (§scaling-vs-growth)
Distinguish **growth** (linear, resource-proportional) from **scaling** (exponential throughput at minimal
friction). Before pushing throttle, **nail it then scale it** — perfect unit economics and clear the
bottleneck, because scaling a broken model just doubles the liability. Apply **theory of constraints**: growth
is dictated by the *single* tightest bottleneck — **founder** (10/10 times it's you), **supply** (usually a
talent bottleneck in services), or **demand** (not enough leads). Misidentifying it is destructive (a supply-
constrained business "fixing" a plateau with a new product just injects complexity). **Take disproportionate
action on the ONE constraint.**

### 2 — EXTRACT THE FOUNDER (§systems-operations, §hiring-team)
Toward **scale-zero** (the 3-month test: step away 3 months, it still grows). Run a **time study** (log every
15 min for a week) → convert each item to a *project/process/person* → triage green/yellow/red. Trust the
**80% rule** (someone at 80% with 100% focus beats a split you) and hand off via **Shadow → Supervise →
Support**. Write **SOPs as if-this-then-that decision trees** with autonomy thresholds so staff stop pulling
you back in. Hire an **operating head (COO) before a co-founder**, use the **14-day A-player litmus**, and
attract A-players with **uncapped earning + infrastructure leverage** (money attracts, culture keeps).

### 3 — GET THE UNIT ECONOMICS RIGHT (§economics-leverage)
Hold the **80% gross-margin floor** for human-delivered service (the margin is the padding that absorbs weaker
new hires as you scale). Target **30-day CAC payback** so the float self-funds acquisition (pull cash forward
with pay-in-full incentives / layaway). Read **LTV:CAC by humans-in-the-loop**: 3:1 only at "all zero", 6:1
one human, 9:1 two, 12:1 three — win by driving CAC→0 (brand/virality) or LTV→∞ (high-ticket). Pull the **four
leverages**: labor (doing→managing), capital (buy assets/expertise), code (~100% incremental margin), media
(free organic distribution).

### 4 — FOCUS & LEAD (§leadership-focus)
**1-1-1 rule** — one product, one avatar, one channel until $1M. **"More" is the highest risk-adjusted move**
— once something works, do more of *it*; cap yourself to one genuinely new thing a year. At scale the founder
is paid for **quality of judgment, not quantity of work** (a decision is "to cut off" — deliberation time is
the real cost). Lead over manage: invest in the hard-to-measure long game; be the **emotional regulator** when
things break.

### 5 — AVOID THE SCALING KILLERS (§failures)
Name the traps: **premature scaling / overexpansion** (double overhead + debt + thinner talent), **complexity
as the failure vector** (simplify + outsource to specialists), **growth-at-all-costs** under investor pressure
(finite mindset in an infinite game), and the **leaky-bucket acquisition treadmill**. **Retention is the
scaling engine** — moving churn 20%→10% *doubles* LTV; churn concentrates in months 1–3, so the highest-ROI
move is onboarding people *past day 90*. And the durable **moat is brand**, not first-party data.

## Output contract
Return, in order:
1. **Scale vs grow + the constraint** — is the model nailed, and which single bottleneck (founder/supply/demand) is binding.
2. **Founder extraction / org** — the delegation + SOP + hire moves toward scale-zero.
3. **Unit economics** — margin floor, CAC payback, the LTV:CAC ratio required at this human-in-loop level, leverage pulled.
4. **Focus** — the 1-1-1 / "more-not-new" call and the one thing to take disproportionate action on.
5. **Risk** — the scaling killer most likely to bite, and the retention/brand move that de-risks it.

## Constraints (what NOT to do)
- **Never scale a model that isn't nailed** — perfect unit economics + clear the bottleneck first; scaling amplifies liability.
- **Never work the 2nd/3rd-best problem** — identify the single tightest constraint and take disproportionate action on it.
- **Never build a business that requires the founder** — that's a job you can't quit; drive to scale-zero (3-month test).
- **Never underprice below the 80% gross-margin floor or ignore CAC payback** — the margin is your scaling padding; 30-day payback self-funds growth.
- **Never apply a flat 3:1 LTV:CAC to a human-delivered business** — raise the required ratio per human in the loop (6:1/9:1/12:1).
- **Never chase "new" over "more", or diversify before $1M** — 1-1-1; one new thing a year; new rarely works and drains resources.
- **Never treat retention as secondary** — it's the scaling engine; onboard past day 90 before chasing raw churn.
- **Never fabricate org-design/RACI, board/financing/M&A, or benchmark methodology** — the corpus is thin there; numbers are single-source claims. Offer/funnel math lives in `sales-funnels`.
