# Project Management Knowledge Base

## §methodologies

- Match the method to the context, not the fashion — almost all real-world PM has converged on **hybrid**; you'll find combinations of ~5 broad types in the wild (src: PM 6720c335, src: PM 7adce503). Assess *how much* agility a project benefits from before choosing (PRINCE2's "agileometer": is scope flexible? add/remove/replace features?) (src: PM 879c6f04).
- **Waterfall (predictive/linear):** heavy upfront planning, standardization, controls to eliminate variation. Fits predictable/physical work (construction) with rigid, well-defined scope. Trade-off: no short feedback cycles → historically over time/budget, poor at mid-project change (src: PM e12bfc8c, src: PM 879c6f04).
- **Agile/Scrum (adaptive):** customer-centric, iterative, working software over docs, respond to change over plan (4 manifesto values, 2001). Fits complex/volatile IT where requirements evolve; excels in **single-team** settings. Trade-off: struggles to scale to multi-team/multi-product with cross-dependencies and to give execs portfolio-level financial/timeline data; risk of "cargo-cult" ceremony-following that loses the flexibility that was the point (src: PM e12bfc8c, src: PM 879c6f04, src: PM fbb8546b).
- **Kanban:** the corpus does **not** define it as a standalone method — only references Kanban-*style boards* as visual task-pipeline trackers (src: PM 33c19bf2). Treat deep Kanban (WIP-flow, pull, cadence) as a gap here.
- **Hybrid / "structured agility":** wraps an agile delivery engine (Scrum) in structured governance — team autonomy + psychological safety underneath, top-down budget/compliance/risk control above (fits VUCA/BANI environments). Trade-off: culture clash (autonomy vs. command-and-control) and high cognitive load on the PM as "agent orchestrator" (src: PM 879c6f04, src: PM 33b03009). PMP expects fluency across predictive/adaptive/hybrid (src: PM d4334e31).
- Specialist variants: **FDD** (feature-driven, values written docs over daily meetings, suits larger teams, leans on lead devs) (src: PM db99d0b1); **TDD** (tests written first *are* the executable definition of done; suits high-reliability, long-lived, multi-dev codebases) (src: PM 173481ee).

## §planning-estimation

- **Scope** = breadth/depth of ambition (physical deliverables or range of work). Always pair scope with **exclusions** (what it will NOT do) and get the sponsor/client to formally sign off the baseline (src: PM b7ca7fe1, src: PM 05b644f9). Under structured agility, distinguish **baseline changes** (affect business justification → formal change control) from **detailed changes** (small refinements handled inside iterations by re-prioritizing/trading same-size user stories) (src: PM 879c6f04).
- **WBS** decomposes scope hierarchically (by activity or by product); it is the foundation for everything downstream — derive resource requirements per lowest-tier work package, then convert to a **Cost Breakdown Structure** by costing each element (src: PM 05b644f9, src: PM 6720c335).
- **Estimating** is one of the hardest disciplines — humans are over-optimistic and biased. Core rule: **never estimate once — estimate at least twice** by different methods (bottom-up vs top-down; expert vs parametric) and reconcile the gap (src: PM 05b644f9, src: PM 3b5404d9). Six techniques: order-of-magnitude (top-down, rounded, early business case); reference-class/analogy (past comparables, adjust for inflation); parametric ("rule of thumb" unit × volume); bids/tenders; catalogues/pricing tables; pilots/prototypes (measure actual, project full-scale) (src: PM 05b644f9). Harden with a **red-team review** — keep the smartest people out of the estimate, then task them to tear it apart or build a parallel one (src: PM 05b644f9).
- **Contingency:** always add it, but never one flat % for the whole project (crude, small-projects-only). Tailor per workstream by uncertainty profile — e.g. ~10% on familiar tech, ~40% on novel/off-the-shelf-less work — then roll up. (PMI calls this "reserves.") (src: PM 05b644f9).
- **Dependencies:** manage internal (task sequence) and external (weather, parallel org initiatives). Unidentified dependencies — especially on shared specialist resources — are a leading bottleneck; map them (RACI/dependency matrix) (src: PM b7ca7fe1, src: PM eb101211).
- **Critical path** = the dependent-task sequence setting the minimum timeline. Optimize with **resource leveling** (resource-limited: shift/delay tasks for an even profile → *moves* the critical path/deadline) vs **resource smoothing** (time-limited: move tasks only within float → does *not* affect critical path). Pragmatists do both; allocation is iterative (src: PM 6720c335).
- **Triple constraint (scope/time/cost) + quality** are in constant tension — manage by **trade-off**: more scope ⇒ more time/cost/risk or lower quality; faster ⇒ descope or add resources. Answer demands with "**yes, if…**" not a blank-check yes or a hostile no (src: PM a7153236). Quality is not a nice-to-have: build it in via **Quality Design** (define expectations/criteria), **QA** (proactive, process-driven prevention: workflows, upskilling, audits, PDCA), **QC** (reactive inspection before handover). Invest in cost-of-conformance to avoid the far higher cost-of-non-conformance (rework, external defects) (src: PM fe06c380).

## §execution-tracking

- **Sprints/iterations:** short time-boxes, typically **2 weeks / 10 working days**. Size the backlog in a consistent unit (story points, hours, or task counts). Track daily with **burndown/burnup charts** vs an "ideal line"; above the ideal = behind, below = ahead (src: PM 500afab8). Close each sprint with a **review** ("fit for purpose?") then a **retrospective** (trends, estimate accuracy, lessons) (src: PM 500afab8, src: PM fbb8546b).
- **Daily standup / daily scrum:** brief sync to update progress, surface what's helping/slowing the team, spot plateaus (= impediments). Build chart-updating into the standup so tracking never goes stale (src: PM 500afab8, src: PM 2819f393). Not universal — FDD meets far less often, preferring written docs (src: PM db99d0b1).
- **Task boards** (physical cards → digital Kanban → Jira/Trello/Azure DevOps) give visual transparency of status, dependencies, bottlenecks (src: PM 33c19bf2, src: PM 2819f393). **WIP limits matter:** a "flatline at the top" of a burndown (nothing finishing early in the sprint) signals over-planning or ignored WIP limits spreading the team too thin — pull in smaller, well-defined tasks (src: PM 2819f393).
- **Status reporting:** small projects report informally/orally; as scale grows, put it on the record (audit trail, RAG/traffic-light status, signed). Modern agile favors **live dashboards** senior leaders "pull" on demand over pushed weekly highlight reports; the **project canvas** puts critical business-justification on one page (src: PM b7ca7fe1, src: PM 879c6f04). Remember a dashboard is only an interim step to a *decision* — the value is the insight and corrective action it enables (src: PM fbb8546b).
- **Project controls** = an integrated suite that keeps delivery on plan. Five core: **baselining/rebaselining, risk/issue management, cost/budget management, progress monitoring & reporting, change control** (plus version/config control, backlog grooming, dependency management). Without a locked baseline there's nothing to measure against. It's a *proactive* mindset (trend-watching, early warning), proportionate to project scale — not administrative box-ticking (src: PM ec19acab).
- **Stakeholder cadence:** communication is the PM's core skill — clear, frequent, and **tailored** to each audience's preferences; over- and under-communicating are both anti-patterns. Interview stakeholders to design the report; keep the format under review on long projects. Reports should carry milestone status (done/underway/overdue), budget/outturn, key risks, resource utilization, and **explicit decisions/escalations needed from the sponsor or board** (src: PM b7ca7fe1, src: PM 7adce503, src: PM c227d43a).

## §risk-change

- **Risk register** is the one mandatory diligence document when you're spending someone else's money/reputation — a *living* management tool and audit trail, not a filed artifact. Four lifecycle sections: (1) **Identify** — unique ID (never reused), originator, short title + full narrative; log every risk (declining to log it makes you culpable if it materializes); (2) **Analyze** — category, likelihood, impact, proximity, consolidated score; (3) **Plan** — mitigation plan + a single named **risk owner** (workstream/team lead, not the PM); (4) **Action & closure** — progress, review dates, closure status (src: PM b7ca7fe1, src: PM 3b5404d9).
- **Assessment cautions:** don't use linear 1–5 scales — worst cases are *exponentially* worse; use 1-2-4-8-16 or 1-10-100-1000. Probability estimates are unreliable (projects are unique, no data). Look beyond likelihood×impact to **proximity** (time/space) and **causal triggers/circumstances** (src: PM 3b5404d9).
- **Responses:** risk ≠ always avoid — it's *calculated* risk-taking (opportunities enable innovation). Mitigate (lower likelihood/impact), contingency-plan ("if-then"), or transfer (insurance/contract) — but **transferred ≠ removed**: it can still bite if the contract is poorly drafted or the counterparty fails; even uncontrollable external risks (economy, pandemic) should be monitored for early signs (src: PM 3b5404d9).
- **Scope creep** is the PM's "nemesis" and a direct uncompensated cost — control it upfront (exclusions, signed baseline) and via a formal **change process**. The PM's job is governance, not personally approving/denying: simple projects = sponsor hears requester + PM evaluation and records a decision; complex = a **design authority group** assesses each change against baseline cost/schedule/risk/benefit. Default response: *"You can have it if it's approved through the proper process."* When you must refuse, use a **Noble Objection** — never a "naked no"; clothe it in a clear "because" (src: PM c227d43a, src: PM a7153236, src: PM 05b644f9).
- **Contingency buffers ("reserves"):** four approaches — whole-project flat (crude), workstream-specific (recommended: 10%→40% by risk), task-by-task roll-up (precise but heavy admin; precision ≠ accuracy), or a balanced hierarchical placement at the WBS levels where real uncertainty sits (src: PM 05b644f9).

## §delivery-closeout

- **Acceptance criteria / Definition of Done:** flow from Quality Design; in adaptive/hybrid, set collaboratively in quality workshops. A weak/ignored DoD causes an end-of-sprint "waterfall" drop (testing/review/deploy left to the last minute) — the DoD must include **shipability** and progress must track backlog-item *completion*, not task busywork. In TDD the passing tests *are* the DoD (src: PM fe06c380, src: PM 2819f393, src: PM 173481ee).
- **Handover/sign-off** is one of five mandatory deliverables — formalizes transfer to the "beneficial owner" and proves commitments were met. For software it follows a testing ladder (unit → integration → system → **User Acceptance Testing**): real end-users test in a real environment because experts already know how it's "supposed" to work and won't find the natural-use faults (src: PM b7ca7fe1, src: PM fe06c380). Close with a **project closure report** (summary, outcomes vs. predicted benefits, actual-vs-baseline performance, lessons, board/sponsor/client approval, version control) (src: PM b7ca7fe1).
- **Lessons learned / retrospectives:** the value is in the *participation and shared learning*, not the filed document. Don't summarize-and-shelve into a spreadsheet — **embed lessons in the live data** (schedule/cost/benefits) so the causal context ("why this slipped") is preserved, and run reviews continuously throughout the life cycle, not just at the end (src: PM 1ce00375, src: PM b7ca7fe1, src: PM 500afab8).
- **Benefits realization:** a benefit = a *measurable* improvement, *perceived as positive by a stakeholder*, that *contributes to a strategic objective*. **Start with the end in mind** — identify the 1–3 real benefits *before* initiating, then design the project to deliver them. Starting the project first and then hunting benefits to justify the spend is "benefit fraud." Because single projects deliver *outputs*, longer-term *outcomes/benefits* are managed one level up in programs/portfolios with a consistent benefits framework (src: PM c3d7238c, src: PM eb101211).

## §people-communication

- **Roles:** **Sponsor** — senior leader who funds, approves baseline decisions, sets vision, removes org roadblocks (≈ PRINCE2 Senior Responsible Officer / Senior Financial Officer for financial accountability). **PM** — the hands-on "driver": plan/execute/control daily, resolve blockers, balance competing stakeholder demands. **Product Owner** (Scrum) — represents client/end-user, owns and prioritizes the backlog (src: PM 6584cc3f, src: PM 05b644f9, src: PM db99d0b1, src: PM 33b03009).
- **Servant leadership over command-and-control:** teams of skilled specialists respond to being supported, upskilled, and empowered — that builds psychological safety, morale, shared ownership and higher-quality work; command-and-control is rarely appropriate in PM (src: PM c227d43a, src: PM 879c6f04). Build teams deliberately: clarify purpose/roles, set ground rules, then strengthen trust via normalized constructive feedback (src: PM e6517381).
- **Delegation:** shed the DIY mindset — value your time as a finite resource with opportunity cost; if your effective hourly value exceeds the cost of delegating a task, delegate it (or delete it) and free energy for higher-level work (src: PM 5b34f783, src: PM ca90636e). Use a **RACI/RACY matrix** (Responsible, Accountable/Authority, Consulted, Informed) to allocate and communicate ownership; sense-check it — one accountable owner per task, no one overloaded (a "key-person risk" and quality hazard) (src: PM 6720c335).
- **Managing internal teams vs. clients:** internal team management is relationship-driven (onboarding → workload balancing → performance/individual management → graceful offboarding); engage the team in *building* the plan (more brains, deeper buy-in) (src: PM 6720c335, src: PM e6517381). Client/sponsor management is strategic influence + boundary-setting, needing the **gravitas** to speak truth to power when expectations clash with reality; it runs on credibility, empathy, and tailored visual reporting (src: PM 33b03009, src: PM b7ca7fe1). The corpus frames the PM→consultant shift as driver→navigator (owns advice, not delivery) (src: PM 33b03009). AI note: PMs increasingly act as "agent orchestrators" running parallel AI-augmented workstreams (src: PM ee49ae9d, src: PM fbb8546b).

## §failures

- Most projects fail not from poor *planning* but from poor *control* — no baseline, deviations unnoticed until they escalate past recovery, and the project is cancelled or (worse) continues and fails (src: PM ec19acab).
- At portfolio level, the **#1 cause is unidentified dependencies** — especially on shared specialist resources that bottleneck scheduling (src: PM eb101211).
- **Anti-patterns** (behaviors with surface appeal but adverse outcomes; term from software eng.): scope creep (no change control); command-and-control leadership; **over-planning / "death by planning"** (no room for contingency/creativity); ignoring stakeholder feedback (→ the wrong-but-well-built solution + expensive rework); unrealistic deadlines; **resource over-allocation** (stress, mistakes, burnout, quality deficits); **metric fixation / analysis paralysis** (data for data's sake distracts from value); "fire drill" tedium-then-panic cadence; and **one-size-fits-all** method reuse instead of tailoring to context (src: PM c227d43a, src: PM 2819f393).

## Gaps / honest caveats

- **Kanban as a method, pure-Scrum ceremony mechanics** (roles, exact sprint events), **CPM math/float mechanics**, and **ICE/PIE-style prioritization or A/B statistics** are not covered — defer to dedicated sources.
- **Agency-specific:** no formal **client onboarding** or **SOW** framework in the corpus. What exists: contract/pricing risk — **fixed-price** shifts all delivery risk to the contractor (price accurately + contingency + margin; never cheap for the client) vs **time-and-materials** (billed on actual hours; avoid the "day-rate trap" — sell expertise/value, not time; watch for uncosted extras → bill as **contract variations**); and **stage-gate / project gateways** to release budget in tranches only as prior work is validated — the nearest thing to milestone billing (src: PM 05b644f9, src: PM 33b03009, src: PM 3b5404d9). No milestone-invoicing templates or client-CRM/onboarding process.
- Numbers (2-week sprints, 10%/40% contingency, "#1 cause") are source claims, directional — validate per engagement.
