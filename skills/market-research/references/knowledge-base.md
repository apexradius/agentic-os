## §competitor-analysis

- **Define the arena wide, not by product category.** A boba shop's real competition isn't just tea houses — you "compete for mouths," the customer's whole *thirst/mood profile*; a physical social space competes on "authentic in-person community," so gaming cafés and VR venues aren't its competitors at all `[MR 137dd0f4, MR 06a37d52]`. Narrow competitor lists are a strategic blind spot.
- **The disruptor blind spot.** Execution-focused ("exploit") leaders systematically miss outside/adjacent disruptors — Blockbuster's CEO called Netflix "not even on the radar" because a short-term operational lens made it invisible `[MR a6187733]`. Watch the periphery, not just look-alikes.
- **Market-triangle segmentation** (mass markets): map rivals by volume × price × exclusivity — high-volume "basics" at the base (biggest TAM), seasonal "current fashion" mid, scarce "trend" at the tip (smallest, priciest). H&M got "squeezed in the middle" once UNIQLO owned the base and ZARA the tip `[MR 2a07402c]`.
- **Strategy Canvas** (Blue Ocean, Kim & Mauborgne) = the notebook's named tool for competitor comparison — plot your value curve against a rival's to see where you converge vs. diverge; it's an *analytical* tool, not a design tool `[MR 7cd62c71]`.
- **Boomerang experiment**: study a competitor's product AND interview *their* customers to harvest what works/fails in their value proposition — Tesla learned its buyers weren't the same customers as rivals' `[MR 65e96d3f]`.
- HONEST GAP: the corpus has **no step-by-step teardown template** (offer/pricing/funnel/ad/content dissection) and **no SWOT method at all** — it teaches moats and canvases instead. Don't fabricate a teardown checklist from it.

## §market-research

- **Validate demand BEFORE building — never build first.** Building an MVP as the first act is the worst move: if it flops you can't tell whether the solution is broken or the need never existed. Spend the first ~12-week sprint proving customer desirability (jobs/pains/gains), not shipping product `[MR 1c2c5182, MR 76e64a95]`. "The MVP is one of ~20 experiments, definitely not the first" `[MR a6187733]`.
- **Levels of evidence (Say → Do), 5 rungs** — raise evidence as you raise spend `[MR 65e96d3f, MR 76e64a95]`: L1 interviews/surveys (light *say*); L2 react to a visual artifact/storyboard (stronger *say*); L3 light call-to-action in an anonymous space — ad click, landing-page email (light *do*); L4 skin-in-the-game — refundable deposit, B2B **Letter of Intent** (non-binding but a real signature), co-creation workshop (strong *do*); L5 irrefutable — Wizard-of-Oz (humans behind "automation," e.g. Fireflies charging for AI that was two people), pop-up, real pre-sale (Tesla Model 3: 325k reservations). **Volume never changes the level** — a million ad clicks is still L3.
- **Run cheap experiments first; limit the blast radius.** Early innovation is *cheap* (often just team salary, not R&D capex) — make small bets, only scale spend as evidence accumulates `[MR fadf5a0c, MR 76e64a95]`. A thriving pipeline runs ~150–200 ideas/yr and competes on *testing speed* `[MR d19f44e7]`.
- **Four risk axes to score every idea** (Innovation Project Scorecard): **desirability** (value prop, jobs/pains/gains, acquisition, retention), **feasibility** (can we build/scale it), **viability** (pricing, will they pay, cost/profit), **adaptability** (market timing, environment, regulation) `[MR 76e64a95]`.
- **TAM is not a price lever.** Cutting price makes a product *more accessible* but does NOT enlarge the market — GoPro wrongly believed cheaper Hero models would expand TAM from athletes to everyone `[MR abbd07e3]`. Size TAM to your model's scale need: Regis needed 400–500 sites/concept, so the $30-haircut TAM was too small — they went low-end walk-in for "a big enough TAM" `[MR d89527e8]`.
- **Big vs. niche.** Startups can't chase mass markets day one — enter a niche "so small incumbents ignore it or so new no leader exists" `[MR 1da741c2]`. Strategic fork: scale-first (Maersk, must re-win revenue yearly) vs. predictability-first (recurring/lock-in); best is both (Novo Nordisk diabetes lock-in = ~20× Maersk's market value on far lower revenue, from revenue *resilience*) `[MR 6810360f]`.
- **Niches are expandable — test, don't assume a cap.** Three growth levers: (1) solve more user problems (common complaints = unmet demand), (2) refine form factor/use case (Insta360 "Go": 20g tap-to-record unlocked parents/pets), (3) lower the entry barrier via innovation/cost `[MR 1da741c2]`.
- **Read maturity/saturation signals:** consumer apathy (grooming grows 1–2%/yr, people don't switch until a bad experience) `[MR d89527e8]`; store growth outpacing customer growth → consolidation into scaled survivors (LA bubble tea: need ~3 shops for your own distribution, ~10 for top ingredients) `[MR 137dd0f4]`; product homogeneity → destructive price war (TVs, monitors, robot vacuums — "who sells the same thing cheapest without going bankrupt") `[MR 1da741c2]`.
- **Timing & the S-curve.** Disruption plays out slowly because infrastructure + behavior lag — carriage→car took ~30 yrs (unreliable cars, no paved roads) `[MR ed1d486a]`. Modern tech moves faster via **accessibility shocks** that spike competitiveness overnight: graphical web browser (1990), AWS (2002, infra on a credit card), OpenAI API (2023) `[MR 7970bf1e]`. Scale when the macro window aligns (Popeyes: low rates + fried-chicken resurgence + untapped demand) `[MR 60473649]`.
- HONEST GAP: **no TAM/SAM/SOM sizing formulas, no primary/secondary-research taxonomy, no sample-size math.** TAM is discussed only conceptually.

## §audience-research

- **Customer profile = Jobs / Pains / Gains** (right side of the Value Proposition Canvas): jobs = what they're trying to get done; pains = obstacles, risks, frustrations before/during/after; gains = desired outcomes/benefits `[MR 384a6914, MR 2ea4ff1f]`.
- **"Say" ≠ "Do."** Voice-of-Customer, surveys, open interviews only capture what people *claim* — a customer who says "I'd buy it" reveals truth only under skin-in-the-game (L3–L5) `[MR 65e96d3f, MR 76e64a95]`. Teams that stopped at "say" evidence killed projects once they reached "do" `[MR 65e96d3f]`.
- **Observe behavior, don't only ask.** Steve Jobs hung out in the Apple Store watching how people used devices — pair interviews with observation `[MR 384a6914]`.
- **Structured customer-profile interviews** beat unstructured (which yield scattered, un-comparable data; "5 of 5 had problem X" collapses to "5 of 17" once you normalize) `[MR d3287468, MR 384a6914]`. Method: (1) pre-map a generic profile of jobs/pains/gains (30–40 stickies); (2) read each aloud — "Is this you?" — validate/kill/tweak; (3) **forced specificity** ("do you order *two large coffees to go*?" not "do you buy coffee?") — over-specifying makes the customer *correct you*, which surfaces the deepest insight; (4) capture *their* ranking of top jobs/pains/gains; run ~15–20 to detect patterns `[MR 384a6914, MR 2ea4ff1f]`.
- **Output is a decision, not notes** — a before/after profile shift (e.g. one gain that 75% flagged) makes the leadership recommendation traceable and un-waffle-able `[MR 2ea4ff1f, MR 384a6914]`.

## §positioning

- **Value innovation / Blue Ocean**: make competitors irrelevant by realigning utility, price, cost. **Four Actions (ERRC)**: which factors to **Eliminate**, **Reduce** below standard, **Raise** above standard, **Create** that the industry never offered `[MR a6187733, MR 7cd62c71]`.
- **Escape the price war by shifting the business model, not the feature.** Schneider Electric moved from selling microgrid *components* (high-capex, risky) to **"Microgrid-as-a-Service"** — partner funds the capex, customer gets a subscription dashboard + SLA, battle shifts from price to *risk elimination* (explicitly analogized to AWS removing the need to buy servers) `[MR b39e8750]`. Same pattern: Hilti tools→service `[MR 6810360f]`.
- **Business-model innovation is the strongest, least-copied moat** — rivals can't reverse-engineer the interlocking mechanics; it needs no IP protection. Marriott's Moxy "superficially copied" CitizenM's model and failed `[MR 26f51607]`. Competing on tech/product alone is "freaking hard" — stay out of that space where possible.
- **Other durable moats seen in corpus:** vertical integration / supply-chain control (Carnegie owned the iron mills; bubble-tea leaders build proprietary upstream supply vs. the 70% buying identical local distributors) `[MR 6461dd77, MR 137dd0f4]`; proximity/scarcity (ZARA owns nearby factories, refreshes monthly, drives full-price urgency) and proprietary material (UNIQLO HEATTECH) `[MR 2a07402c]`; first-mover "fortress hubs" (airlines) and network lock-in (Visa/Mastercard duopoly) `[MR 627c3470, MR 6ed8a09f]`.
- **What is NOT a moat:** raw data (Wish bragged "data-science moat," collapsed on generic product quality; MyFitnessPal data had "no inherent business value") `[MR 4da6a687, MR 2a3145da]`; and category creation alone — GoPro created the action-cam category, then near-died from SKU overload + stagnation. **"Product is a moat only with humility"** `[MR 1da741c2, MR abbd07e3]`.
- **Craft the value prop with a Customer Value Scene** — a 3-frame storyboard: (1) moment of need/pain, (2) how they solve it *today* (friction), (3) how your offer solves it *tomorrow* (clearly better) — take it to customers before building `[MR 7970bf1e]`.

## §failures

- **Ideas don't matter (early); they're usually wrong.** At the search stage "good ideas and bad ideas look virtually the same" on paper — no pre-selection, let evidence kill or fund `[MR fadf5a0c, MR a6187733]`.
- **Falling in love with the solution/technology.** Teams default to their tech instead of the customer's jobs/pains/gains — one built an **$800k app** and only then found the customer had *zero* interest `[MR 2ea4ff1f, MR 1c2c5182]`.
- **Confirmation bias & no upfront success criteria.** Humans are biased; unstructured interviews invite cherry-picking ("cheating on discovery is like cheating in parachute-packing class"). Define hypotheses, metrics, and pass/fail thresholds *before* touching customers — else you get **"pilot purgatory"** and resource-draining **zombie projects** validated by any polite comment `[MR 384a6914, MR 1e653b0e]`.
- **Analysis paralysis / "awesome spreadsheets."** Weeks perfecting an ICP or a 5-yr business case before one customer conversation is waste (20% froze 2–3 wks perfecting an ICP) `[MR 7ccd6870, MR 7ccd6870]`. Scott Cook (Intuit): *"For every one of our failures, we had spreadsheets that looked awesome"* — a spreadsheet is "making a fantasy explicit," not evidence `[MR fadf5a0c, MR 76e64a95]`. **Rule: design like you're right, test like you're wrong** — sketch fast, then run cheap experiments `[MR 65e96d3f, MR fadf5a0c]`.
- **Researching without a decision to make.** Interviews must *inform a decision*; a pilot with no defined start/end/criteria "isn't on track for success" `[MR 1e653b0e]`. Success is defined externally: a value prop customers care about, embedded in a business model that scales profitably — you iterate search↔scale until you find it `[MR bec55620, MR 29615a45]`.
- **Corporate "antibodies" kill adjacencies** — the core (exploit) business instinctively starves anything different ("Osterwalder's law"); new bets need top-down protection, not just bottom-up energy `[MR 3283bbe0, MR 45350542]`.
